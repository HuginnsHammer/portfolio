METHOD / INTEGRITY / INTERPRETATION

Investigate carefully. Report precisely.

Digital forensics depends on more than finding an interesting artifact. A defensible account explains where evidence came from, how it was handled, what the tools showed, and which conclusions remain uncertain.

My working principles

  • Authorization and scope: work only with systems and evidence I am permitted to examine, and define the question before collecting data.
  • Preservation: record source, acquisition method, timestamps, and cryptographic hashes where appropriate. Work from copies when possible.
  • Reproducibility: note tool names and versions, commands, assumptions, and transformations that affect interpretation.
  • Timeline discipline: distinguish source timestamps, capture timestamps, timezone assumptions, and inferred event order.
  • Calibrated conclusions: separate direct observations from interpretations, alternative explanations, and unsupported hypotheses.
  • Privacy: sanitize identifiers and avoid publishing personal, confidential, or operationally sensitive evidence.

Case-note format

When I publish a forensic exercise, the report should include:

  1. Question, scope, authorization, and known constraints.
  2. Evidence inventory, provenance, hashes, and chain-of-custody notes where relevant.
  3. Environment, tool versions, acquisition steps, and reproducible commands.
  4. Artifact and timeline analysis, with references to the evidence supporting each observation.
  5. Findings, confidence, alternative explanations, and limitations.
  6. Conclusion that answers the original question without going beyond the evidence.
Evidence handling note

Examples on this site should use authorized training material, synthetic artifacts, or evidence explicitly approved for publication. A challenge flag or isolated artifact is not, by itself, proof of a broader incident narrative.
